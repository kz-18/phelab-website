Project 01

Utilizing Empirical Ethics to Conceptualize Religion-Based Vaccine Hesitancy in the United States

The proposed project investigates vaccine hesitancy among religious leaders and future healthcare workers in the U.S. through the following aims

Investigate religion-based attitudes towards three types of vaccines among Christian and Jewish community leaders and pre-health students in the Phoenix metropolitan area

Our aim is to use empirical ethics qualitative methods to unpack how religious frameworks, morals, values and perceptions of science underpin the willingness acceptance vs. hesitancy among religious individuals and groups.

Begin curriculum development targeted for pre-health students

Curriculum will be designed to explore religion-based attitudes toward vaccine hesitancy, and how these beliefs and attitudes might impact their professional practices and personal well-being.

Investigate the generalizability of our findings

In order to generalize the findings, this lab will survey a nationally representative sample of healthcare workers in the United States.

Project 02

Characterizing healthcare provider and patient experiences with implementing genomic medicine in a Federally Qualified Health Center

The proposed project investigates experiences of healthcare providers caring for low-resourced patients in a Federally Qualified Health Center in Phoenix, AZ using mixed-methods, cross-sectional case study

Characterize healthcare provider experiences caring for Latino/a patients receiving PRS in a Federally Qualified Health Center (FQHC) in Phoenix, Arizona, with a contextual emphasis on the setting, social determinants, and normative influences that underpin how healthcare providers integrate genomic medicine into patient care.

We will conduct in-person, on-site semi-structured interviews with up to 20 healthcare providers at MPHC that deliver PRS to Latino/a patients as part of eMERGE IV

Characterize Latino/a patient experiences receiving PRS in a Federally Qualified Health Center (FQHC) in Phoenix, Arizona, with a contextual emphasis on the social determinants that shape to the effectiveness and utility of genomic medicine for them.

We will conduct in-person, on-site semi-structured interviews with up to 8 Latino/a patients at MPHC that received PRS as part of eMERGE III and IV
